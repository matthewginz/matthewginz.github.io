Systems portfolio · Matthew Ginzburg

A cross-venue prediction-market research system

Two US prediction-market venues, structurally different data models, and a hard deadline on every row: once a match is played, any price data not logged in real time is gone. This is the collection and research stack built around that constraint — ~27,000 lines of Python, ten SQLite databases, a dozen unattended jobs.

The problem

Prediction markets, priced twice

The same real-world outcome — a striker to score, a team to win in regulation — trades on both Polymarket US and Kalshi, in two independent order books, under settlement rules that don't always agree (one counts extra-time goals, the other doesn't). Kalshi alone lists over a thousand soccer series across ~76 leagues; its own catalog contains copy-paste errors. Polymarket carries a live score and match clock on its event objects; Kalshi has no such field at all.

There is no historical-price endpoint on either side. The system exists to turn that transient, messy, two-venue stream into a clean, queryable record before the information disappears — and to do it correctly enough to run unattended for weeks.

Architecture

Ten databases, deliberately not merged

Three public APIs feed scheduled collectors. The write targets are split by lifecycle and by trust boundary, not lumped into one store — that split is the design.

Solid = written every run. The account ledger and the archive chain are the only route to a published result; the research databases never reach it. The live DB dropped from 445 MB to 760 KB the day tick history moved to the cold archive — it's grown since as the account's still-private, ongoing trading keeps using the same ledger file.

Engineering

Decisions worth explaining

Live / archive split by row growth, not table count

The live database's problem was never how many tables it had — it was unbounded tick-history growth. archive_to_historical.py is a standing process: filter by game_slug, verify the copied row count matches the source exactly, take a full backup, then delete. SQLite's cross-database rollback is tested — a failed archive leaves both files untouched.

Structural market discovery

With 1,000+ Kalshi soccer series and real errors in the venue's own data (a *SPREAD series titled "… Game"), discovery matches on the shape of a real fixture — a "Team vs Team (date)" event sub-title — and cross-checks every ticker against known market-type substrings rather than trusting title text.

Read-only dashboards that can't stall the writer

Monitoring is a local stdlib HTTP server; every database connection it opens is mode=ro, enforced at the file level. A dashboard can never contend with the live collector. No hosted page — the data is in local SQLite a sandboxed page couldn't reach anyway.

Client-side rate limiting, from a real incident

65 rate-limit rejections in one session, root-caused to concurrent workers sharing one client with reactive-only backoff. Fixed with a thread-safe token bucket built into the client — smoothing requests under the ceiling beats bursting then sleeping, and it's faster in wall time.

A hard boundary between paper and real

The funded-account code, order clients, and private keys live in a separate repository and never touch a public remote — a lesson this project learned the direct way. The paper-trading and research databases have no code path into any published result.

Status & honesty

What works, and what didn't

The interesting result of a research project is often a clean negative, and several of these are.

Live The collection stack runs unattended on a schedule. Discovery, tiered capture, archival, and the read-only dashboards work as designed.
Honest zero The cross-venue arb scanner mostly finds no edge net of fees, and reports it as a zero rather than forcing a trade. That is the correct outcome, not a failure of the scanner.
Halted by design A separate genetic strategy-discovery pipeline runs a formal multiple-testing gate (Deflated Sharpe, probability of backtest overfitting). It has halted every campaign so far — which is the gate doing its job, and a result worth having.
In progress A news-signal research layer (open news feeds vs. Kalshi market moves) is being built with the same overfitting discipline.
